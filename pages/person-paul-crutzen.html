Paul Crutzen

1933 – 2021 · Atmospheric chemist

He won the 1995 Nobel Prize in Chemistry for his work on ozone, and popularised the word “Anthropocene” for the age in which humans reshape the Earth.

What they contributed

  • Shared the 1995 Nobel Prize in Chemistry with Mario Molina and F. Sherwood Rowland for identifying how nitrogen oxides erode the ozone layer.
  • With biologist Eugene Stoermer, reintroduced and popularised the term “Anthropocene” in 2000. This is the word used on the Anthropocene page of this site.
  • Directed the Atmospheric Chemistry Department at the Max Planck Institute for Chemistry in Mainz, Germany, from 1980 to 2000.
  • Was among the first scientists to introduce the idea of a “nuclear winter”, the climate effects of large-scale smoke and pollution in the atmosphere.
