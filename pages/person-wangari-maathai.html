Wangari Maathai

1940 – 2011 · Environmental and political activist

She founded the Green Belt Movement in 1977, which encouraged women to plant trees, and in 2004 became the first African woman to win the Nobel Peace Prize.

What they contributed

  • Started the Green Belt Movement in 1977 to counter the deforestation that threatened the livelihoods of farming communities.
  • The movement encouraged women to plant trees in their own areas and spread to other African countries. Britannica says it contributed to the planting of over thirty million trees; other sources give higher figures.
  • Won the 2004 Nobel Peace Prize “for her contribution to sustainable development, democracy and peace”, the first African woman to do so.
  • Was the first woman in East and Central Africa to earn a doctorate in biology, and the first female professor in Kenya.
  • Was elected to Kenya’s National Assembly in 2002 and appointed assistant minister for environment, natural resources and wildlife in 2003.
