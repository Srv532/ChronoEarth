Charles David Keeling

1928 – 2005 · Climate scientist

He measured carbon dioxide in the atmosphere at Mauna Loa, Hawaii, from 1958. The record, called the Keeling Curve, showed that CO₂ was steadily rising.

What they contributed

  • Started the Scripps CO₂ programme in 1956 and ran it until his death in 2005.
  • Began measuring CO₂ at the Mauna Loa weather station in March 1958. The first day’s reading was about 313 parts per million.
  • Discovered the natural seasonal “breathing” of the planet, as plants take up and release CO₂ through the year.
  • Showed that CO₂ was rising because of fossil-fuel burning and land-use change. The plot of his data is the Keeling Curve.
  • The Mauna Loa record is the longest direct record of CO₂ in the atmosphere. His son Ralph Keeling continues the work.
