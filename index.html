Precambrian Supereon

4.6 billion – 541 million years ago

Spanning over 4 billion years, the Precambrian represents the vast, foundational chapter of Earth's history. It begins with the formation of our planet from a hellish, molten state and encompasses the cooling of the crust, the formation of the first oceans, and the origin of life itself. By the end of this supereon, Earth had transformed into a world capable of supporting early, complex multicellular organisms.

Paleozoic Era

541 – 252 million years ago

The Paleozoic Era, meaning "ancient life," marks a time of dramatic biological and geological change. It kicked off with the Cambrian Explosion, an unprecedented burst of evolutionary innovation that introduced most major animal groups. Over the next 300 million years, life conquered the continents, leading to vast forests, early amphibians, and massive insects, culminating tragically in the most devastating mass extinction our planet has ever witnessed.

Mesozoic Era

252 – 66 million years ago

Often referred to as the "Age of Reptiles," the Mesozoic Era is best known as the time when dinosaurs dominated the Earth. Spanning from 252 to 66 million years ago, this era saw the breakup of the supercontinent Pangea, the evolution of the first birds and mammals, and the rise of flowering plants. It ultimately came to a dramatic close with the infamous K-Pg extinction event, triggered by a catastrophic asteroid impact.

  • Mesozoic Era
  • K-Pg Extinction

Cenozoic Era

66 million years ago – Present

The Cenozoic Era, known as the "Age of Mammals," spans the last 66 million years up to the present day. In the wake of the dinosaur extinction, small, furry survivors rapidly evolved and diversified to fill vacant ecological niches across the globe. This era is characterized by the cooling of Earth's climate, repeated ice ages, and ultimately, the evolution of primates, leading to the emergence of modern humans.
